I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Chemical Separations Group at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL). My current work focuses on the molecular-level thermodynamics and properties of molten salts relevant to nuclear fission and fusion technologies.

My research combines statistical mechanics, density functional theory, molecular dynamics, free-energy methods, and machine-learning interatomic potentials to connect molecular interactions with experimentally measurable properties.

My broader research experience spans biomolecular and medical applications—including NMR relaxation and MRI contrast agents—to ionic liquids, molten salts, nuclear energy, and emerging quantum-computing approaches for molecular simulation.

Research

Research directions

My work spans molecular biophysics, statistical mechanics, machine learning, and energy materials, with a common focus on extracting predictive insight from molecular-level simulations.

Molecular biophysics

Statistical Mechanics, NMR & Molecular Biophysics

Molecular theories and simulations of NMR relaxation, complex fluids, and gadolinium-based MRI contrast agents, connecting microscopic molecular motion to experimentally measurable relaxation behavior.

Nuclear materials

Molten Salts & Nuclear Energy

Atomic-scale structure, thermodynamics, transport, and speciation in molten salts relevant to nuclear fission, fusion, and chemical separations.

Simulation + AI

Machine-Learning Interatomic Potentials

Physics-informed use of machine-learning interatomic potentials to extend quantum-mechanical accuracy to the length and time scales required for statistically converged molecular simulations.

Quantum chemistry

Quantum Computing & Electronic Structure

Quantum-classical approaches for challenging electronic-structure problems, including molten salts relevant to tritium chemistry and future fusion blanket materials.
